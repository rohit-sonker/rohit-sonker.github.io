Rohit Sonker

Robotics Ph.D. candidate · Auton Lab, Carnegie Mellon University

I am a Robotics Ph.D. candidate at the Auton Lab, Carnegie Mellon University, advised by Prof. Jeff Schneider.

I build AI agents that make decisions in the physical world: designing experiments, controlling robots and machines, and learning from data that is scarce and expensive to collect. My work brings together reinforcement learning, Bayesian optimization, and large language models, with a focus on methods that hold up outside simulation.

I test these ideas on one of the hardest control problems in science: shaping plasma inside a nuclear fusion reactor. Every experiment is costly, simulators are inaccurate, and the dynamics shift from one run to the next. Methods from my work have run live on the DIII-D tokamak in San Diego.

  • AI for scientific discovery. LLM-guided Bayesian optimization that reads expert notes, experiment logs, and domain knowledge to design better experiments with fewer trials. AuxBO (arXiv 2026)·LLM-based BO (NeurIPS WS 2025)·Multi-Timescale BO (ICML 2025)
  • Reinforcement learning for real systems. Offline and adaptive RL with learned world models: train from historical data, adapt when the dynamics change, and deploy on real hardware. Adaptive Offline RL (NeurIPS 2026)·RL4F Benchmark (NeurIPS 2026)·Offline RL for Rotation Control (L4DC 2026)
  • Robot learning. Dynamics models that infer hidden context on the fly, for robot arms handling changing loads and mobile robots crossing unseen terrain. HiP-RSSM (ICLR 2022)·Terrain-Aware Dynamics (RA-L 2021)
  • Multimodal and agentic models. Language and time-series models for reasoning over scientific data, and post-training LLM agents with reinforcement learning and on-policy distillation.

Previously, I spent over four years shipping production ML and LLM systems. I led the India machine learning team at CS DISCO, building LLM agents for legal discovery, and built clinical trial design and document generation systems for pharma clients at PwC. I hold a dual degree (B.Tech and M.Tech) in Mechanical Engineering from IIT Kanpur, where I worked on robot navigation with Prof. Ashish Dutta.

Dynamics-Informed Adaptive Offline RL for Real-Time Tokamak Plasma Control
R. Sonker, X. Hu, H. Farre, A. Rothstein, J. Chen, E. Kolemen, J. Schneider
Conference on Neural Information Processing Systems (NeurIPS 2026)

Control policies trained entirely from historical data, using a learned world model of plasma dynamics. The policies infer a hidden context and adapt in real time when the dynamics shift. Validated in experiments on the DIII-D tokamak.

arXiv
Bayesian Optimization with Rich Auxiliary Information via LLMs
E. F. Karagözlü*, R. Sonker*, T. Gupta, B. Póczos, J. Schneider
Preprint (arXiv 2026)

Standard Bayesian optimization keeps only an input and a score from each experiment. We use LLMs to read everything else an experiment produces, such as training curves, expert notes, and scientists' logs, and turn it into guidance on where the optimum lies. This beats standard and LLM-based BO on benchmarks and a fusion task.

NeurIPS
Offline Reinforcement Learning for Plasma Control in Nuclear Fusion: Codebase and Benchmark
Y. Fu*, H. Bao*, R. Sonker, X. Hu, A. Venugopal, J. Schneider, J. Chen
Conference on Neural Information Processing Systems (NeurIPS 2026)

RL4F, an open benchmark for offline RL on long-horizon control with many actuators, built from real DIII-D data. It covers four profile-tracking tasks with closed-loop evaluation. Model-based offline RL performs best on most tasks, but no single method wins everywhere.

Offline Reinforcement Learning for Rotation Profile Control in Tokamaks
R. Sonker, H. Farre, J. Chen, A. Rothstein, I. Char, R. Shousha, E. Kolemen, J. Schneider
Learning for Dynamics and Control Conference (L4DC 2026)

Learning a controller with many inputs and outputs when no accurate simulator exists. Probabilistic dynamics models trained on historical data generate rollouts for RL training. The final policy was deployed on the DIII-D tokamak with promising results.

Multi-Timescale Dynamics Model Bayesian Optimization for Plasma Stabilization in Tokamaks
R. Sonker*, A. Capone*, A. Rothstein, H. Farre, E. Kolemen, J. Schneider
International Conference on Machine Learning (ICML 2025)

Bayesian optimization that uses a learned dynamics model to make sequential decisions when data is sparse and every evaluation is expensive. Deployed at the DIII-D National Fusion Facility, where it improved instability avoidance by 117%.
